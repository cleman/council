# WG3 FINAL — Law, risk by profile, alternatives (verified 2026-10-20)
Method: WebSearch summaries only (WebFetch broken); no primary text was opened directly. Grades: VERIFIED (legal text, official site, or several independent sources agree) / CREDIBLE / ALLEGED / CONTRADICTED / UNKNOWN. A point that rests only on law-firm blogs is graded no higher than CREDIBLE.

## 1. Removal risk depends on your status
- **EU/EEA/Swiss citizens.** An expulsion order (OQTF) needs *personal conduct* that is a "real, present and sufficiently serious threat to a fundamental interest of society" (CESEDA L251-1, from EU Directive 2004/38). Committing an offence, or even being convicted, is not enough on its own. Being arrested at a protest without a serious conviction almost certainly does not meet this test. **[VERIFIED rule; CREDIBLE that the risk is low]** — legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/section_lc/LEGITEXT000006070158/LEGISCTA000042771020/ ; Défenseure des droits opinion, 9 July 2019 (juridique.defenseurdesdroits.fr/doc_num.php?explnum_id=19046)
- **Non-EU residents with a residence permit.** Temporary and multi-year cards can be withdrawn or refused for a "threat to public order". The 10-year resident card can only be withdrawn for a *serious* threat. The 2024 immigration law (loi 2024-42) added a duty to respect "republican principles", and a serious breach can cost a permit. It also weakened protection against expulsion after certain convictions. An arrest alone is not automatic grounds, but prefectures check police files (§2). **This is the highest-stakes adult group.** **[VERIFIED law; CREDIBLE on practice]** — legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/article_jo/JORFARTI000049040337 ; gisti.org
- **Asylum seekers.** A prefect can move the claim to the fast-track procedure on "serious threat" grounds (CESEDA L531-27). Losing refugee status requires a very serious final conviction. **[CREDIBLE; this was not re-checked against official sources and the main source is a law firm]**
- **Dual nationals.** They cannot be removed. Stripping of nationality only applies to naturalised people, for terrorism-type offences. **[CREDIBLE]**

## 2. TAJ police file
Being arrested creates an entry in the file even if you are never prosecuted. If the case is dropped, the CPP art. 230-8 rule is that the data receive a *mention* (a note hiding them from administrative background checks) **unless the prosecutor orders deletion**. Deletion is not automatic. Lawyers report that a Cour de cassation ruling of 27 Jan 2026 confirmed there is no automatic deletion. That ruling was not seen. How to act: write to the prosecutor of the place where the case was handled. Ask the Interior Ministry for access to your entry and go to the CNIL if that is blocked. ## 4. Kettling (nasse)
The Conseil d'État ruled on 10 June 2021 (n°444849, Lebon) and struck down the encirclement section of the 2020 policing doctrine (SNMO) because it did not say when the tactic could be used. **Correction:** the Interior Ministry reissued the doctrine on 16 Dec 2021, and encirclement is allowed again. # WG3 UPDATE — Law, risk by profile, alternatives (2026-10-20)
Method: WebSearch (extended) only; WebFetch broken, so facts come from search summaries, many from law-firm blogs (flagged). Updates/corrects Part 3 of prior-brief-2-3.md.

## 1. Removal/residence risk — four distinct regimes (corrects "foreign nationals" lumped together)
- **(a) EU/EEA/Swiss citizens.** No residence permit to withdraw and the 2024 "republican-principles contract" does not apply. Removal (OQTF or expulsion) requires that *personal conduct* be a "menace réelle, actuelle et suffisamment grave pour un intérêt fondamental de la société" (CESEDA L251-1 for OQTF; same test for expulsion). A conviction alone is not enough; after 10 years' lawful residence only "imperative" public-security grounds count. A protest arrest without serious conviction is very unlikely to meet this test. [VERIFIED for the rule; CREDIBLE for low risk] — conseil-etat.fr (CRP 507674, Feb 2026); village-justice.com/articles/eloignement-citoyen-europeen…,52953
- **(b) Non-EU residents with a titre de séjour.** Temporary/multi-year cards can be withdrawn for "menace à l'ordre public"; 10-year carte de résident only for a *serious* threat (CESEDA L432-4 ff.). The 2024 immigration law (loi 2024-42, art. 46) added a contract to respect republican principles: serious breach can mean refusal/withdrawal; it also weakened protections against expulsion after convictions for offences punishable by several years. Decisions must be reasoned and are appealable; arrest alone is not automatic grounds, but the prefecture can consult police files (see TAJ). Still the highest-stakes adult group. [VERIFIED law; CREDIBLE on practice] — legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/article_jo/JORFARTI000049040337 ; gisti.org (ce que change la loi 2024) ; CNCDH opinion A-2024-7
- **(c) Asylum seekers / refugees.** Arrest does not suspend the claim or cancel the attestation, but a prefect can switch the claim to the *accelerated procedure* for "menace grave" (CESEDA L531-27), or seek house arrest/OQTF. Ending refugee status for public-order reasons requires a final conviction for a very serious offence (terrorism or ≥10-year offence) — far beyond typical protest charges. [CREDIBLE; mainly law-firm sources] — kohenavocats.com/demandeur-asile-interpelle-manifestation… ; bpda.assas-universite.fr (OFPRA & ordre public)
- **(d) Dual nationals (French + other).** As French citizens they cannot be removed. Loss of nationality (déchéance) applies only to naturalised persons, for terrorism or attacks on fundamental national interests — not protest offences. Only ordinary criminal/TAJ consequences. [CREDIBLE, Code civil art. 25; no protest-specific case found]

## 2. TAJ (police records file)
An arrest is recorded even without prosecution. After a dismissal (classement sans suite), data are **not automatically deleted**; they are flagged ("mention") so they are hidden from *administrative vetting* — but the flag is often not added automatically and must be requested. Administrative vetting covers private security, airport/port restricted zones, police/justice/sensitive public posts, some childcare roles and naturalisation/residence. Deletion request: registered letter to the **prosecutor of the place of the investigation**; appeal to the president of the chambre de l'instruction; access via the Interior Ministry / CNIL (indirect access). [VERIFIED core (CNIL; CPP art. 230-8)] — cnil.fr/fr/les-refus-dembauche-un-poste-dagent-de-securite… ; village-justice.com/articles/fichier-traitement-des-antecedents-judiciaires-taj-comment-effacer,30921

## 3. Phone in custody (GAV)
- Police take the phone during GAV. **Searching its content**: CJEU 4 Oct 2024 (C-548/21) requires prior review by a court/independent authority except in urgency. **But** Cass. crim. 19 May 2026 (n°25-87.563) and 10 Mar 2026 hold that signing written consent to a search of personal effects also counts as consent to phone exploitation. Practical point: do not sign a consent form without a lawyer. [CREDIBLE; read via law blogs only] — baudelinavocat.fr (CJUE) ; kohenavocats.com/consentement-fouille-exploitation…-19-mai-2026
- **Unlock code**: Cass. Ass. plén. 7 Nov 2022 (n°21-83.146) — refusal can be an offence (Code pénal 434-15-2: up to 3 yrs/€270k) **only if** the phone has active encryption the code unlocks, after a formal request by an authority. Modern smartphones usually meet this. Prosecutions of protesters on this basis: frequency UNKNOWN. [VERIFIED] — service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/actualites/A16210
- LDH advice: leave the phone at home or use a spare; memorise a lawyer/relative's number.

## 4. Kettling (nasse)
Conseil d'État 10 June 2021 (n°444849, LDH & SNJ) annulled the encirclement provision of the 2020 national policing doctrine (SNMO) because it did not define when it may be used; also annulled limits on journalists. # Fact Desk 5 — Legal exposure, stun grenades, Lyon 20 Oct, complaint routes
Method: WebSearch ("extended") summaries only. WebFetch is broken, so no primary text was opened directly. Grades follow charter rule 7.

**1. Joining a demonstration (not organising it).** **VERIFIED:** simply taking part in an *undeclared* demonstration is not an offence. In case no. 21-81.054 (14 June 2022), the Cour de cassation (France's highest court for criminal and civil cases) overturned fines given to protesters in Metz. It held that no law or regulation punishes mere participation. Art. 431-9 of the Code pénal (the criminal code) applies to organisers only. Sources: [Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/juri/id/JURITEXT000045939979), [AEF](https://www.aefinfo.fr/depeche/678582-la-cour-de-cassation-rappelle-que-participer-a-une-manifestation-non-declaree-n-est-pas-une-infraction).

**VERIFIED:** taking part in a *banned* demonstration is a minor offence under art. R644-4, punished by a 4th-class fine. That means €135 if paid at the standard rate, €375 if the increased rate applies, and up to €750 if a court decides. The demonstration counts as banned only if a prefectoral order (*arrêté*) banned it in advance. Police warnings given on the day do not make it banned. Sources: [justice.fr](https://www.justice.fr/themes/manifestations-illicites), [Tricaud](https://tricaudavocats.fr/contravention-de-participation-a-une-manifestation-interdite-extension-du-domaine-de-la-repression/). **CONTRADICTED:** an XR legal guide says it is a 5th-class fine of up to €1,500.

**VERIFIED, and the main real risk:** under art. 431-4, staying on purpose in an *attroupement* (an unlawful gathering) after the *sommations* (formal police orders to disperse) is a criminal offence. The penalty is 1 year in prison and a €15,000 fine. It rises to 3 years if you cover your face to avoid identification, and 5 years plus €75,000 if you carry a weapon. Source: [Kohen](https://kohenavocats.com/attroupement-sommations-disperser-paris-peine-defense-2026/).

  - a criminal complaint to the police or the procureur (prosecutor). This is the only route that can lead to prosecution.
  
  The LDH can help with any of these.

## 9. What the evidence says about 17 October

**The government's case that LFI was behind the unrest**, with the evidence it cites:
- Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez called the participation of LFI elected officials "irresponsible" (28 Sept, CORROBORATED quote).
- Around 1 Oct, the Prime Minister's office (Matignon) blamed "LFI and its far-left proxies" for an "organised manoeuvre" (CORROBORATED as a statement).
- It cites a 28 Sept intelligence note from the Paris police (DRPP), first reported by *Le Figaro* (right-leaning). The note describes far-left groups "instrumentalising" pupil unions (CREDIBLE that the note exists).
- *Le JDD* (right-wing, Bolloré-owned) reported that LFI local councillors are under investigation for transporting fireworks and mortars. Its sources are anonymous (ALLEGED).
- After 17 Oct, Nuñez said many far-left militants had been identified (CREDIBLE as a statement).
- Not in dispute: LFI openly supports the movement and shares its experience of running school blockades (CORROBORATED).
- Sources: [franceinfo explainer](https://www.franceinfo.fr/societe/education/blocage-des-lycees/pourquoi-lfi-est-elle-accusee-par-le-gouvernement-d-etre-a-l-origine-du-mouvement-lyceen-et-de-l-instrumentaliser_8221240.html) (public broadcaster), [JDD](https://www.lejdd.fr/politique/mobilisation-lyceenne-des-elus-lfi-impliques-dans-la-preparation-des-blocus-185739).

- franceinfo read the DRPP note. It covers only Seine-Saint-Denis: 27 actions, 6 of them with left-wing trade unionists or elected officials present.

## 10. Where the council disagreed

- **Abstainer vs Local Realist:** should someone who supports (c) stay home until 5 Nov, or decide city by city and hour by hour? The crux: how much control you have over your position in a crowd when kettling is lawful.
- **Abstainer vs Executor:** can a declared event on 24 Oct ever be a "Go"? The crux: whether it gives any leverage that 5 Nov does not (UNKNOWN).
- **Minors during the holidays:** the "Avoid" rating partly relied on a claim that grenades were re-authorised on 16 Oct, which is now graded UNKNOWN. The chair kept "Avoid" on the other grounds, which are weaker. In term time: "Caution", with no data.
- **Contrarian vs First Principles:** if 49.3 is used, does the size of the strike change the text? There is no French evidence either way.
- **Outsider vs Expansionist:** does strand (d) need street protest at all? The crux: whether the inquiry takes testimony from the public.

## 12. Glossary

- **49.3:** a constitutional article that passes a text without a vote unless a censure motion succeeds.
- **ASSD / GMD:** a launched stun grenade / a hand-thrown dispersal grenade.
- **Arrêté préfectoral:** a local order from the State, such as a ban or a curfew.
- **Censure:** a vote to bring down the government.
- **CESEDA:** the code governing foreigners' entry and residence.
- **Défenseur des droits:** the national rights ombudsman.
- **Devoir de réserve:** a public servant's duty of restraint.
- **DRPP:** the Paris police intelligence directorate.
- **IGPN / IGGN:** the police / gendarmerie inspectorates.
- **Intersyndicale:** the joint front of the main unions.
- **LDH:** the Human Rights League.
- **LFI:** La France insoumise, a left-wing party.
- **Lycéen:** a high-school pupil.
- **La Cimade / GISTI:** migrants' rights groups.
- **Mission:** a chapter of the budget.
- **Nasse:** kettling, where police encircle a crowd.
- **NPA / UNEF:** a far-left party / a student union.
- **Parcoursup:** the university admissions platform.
- **Part 1 (of the PLF):** the revenue half of the budget bill. If it is rejected, the whole bill falls.
- **PLF:** the annual budget bill.
- **Préfecture / préfet:** the State's local office / its representative.
- **Procureur:** the public prosecutor.
- **RT:** the territorial police intelligence service.
- **SNCF:** the national railway.
- **Sommations:** formal orders to disperse.
- **TAJ:** the police records file.
- **TZR:** a roaming substitute teacher.
- **USL:** a high-school pupils' union.
