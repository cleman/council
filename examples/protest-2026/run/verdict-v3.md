# Should I join the autumn 2026 protests? Council 2 verdict, v3 (20 Oct 2026)

**How to read the grades.** No primary text was opened: every fact comes from search-engine summaries (section 11). So the top grade is **CORROBORATED**, not "verified":
- **CORROBORATED:** several independent summaries agree, including an official or legal source where one exists.
- **CREDIBLE:** one solid source, or several sources that agree but are not independent.
- **ALLEGED:** a single or interested source.
- **CONTRADICTED:** better evidence says otherwise.
- **UNKNOWN:** no usable evidence.

Sources are tagged by leaning where relevant, whichever side it is.

## The 60-second version

- There is no single movement. There are **four strands**, each with its own demands, dates and risks.
- **Staying home is a legitimate choice.** It needs no justification.
- If you share one strand's demands and an arrest would cost you little, the lowest-risk street option is a **declared daytime march**. Walk in the main body and leave before it disperses.
- **Your profile can override everything else.** Non-EU temporary-card holders, asylum seekers and minors during the holidays should stay off the street.
- Several key facts can't be known today. **Section 8** says what to check, and when.

## 1. Bottom line

The answer depends on whether you share a strand's demands and what an arrest would cost you. The council takes no side on any demand.
- If you share none of the demands, or an arrest would cost you dearly, staying home or acting off the street is sound.
- If you share one and your risk is low, the lowest-risk street option is a declared, daytime march. Walk in the main body and leave before it disperses.

## 2. Your first step

Write down "I want [outcome] from [decision-maker] by [date]", then match it to one strand:
- **(a) Schools:** the lycéen (high-school pupils') movement, led by pupil unions such as the USL. Its demands are teachers, building repairs and the end of Parcoursup (CREDIBLE).
- **(b) Cost of living:** a Gilets jaunes coordination, on Saturday 24 and Saturday 31 Oct (CORROBORATED). It has no formal platform.
- **(c) Wages, public services, budget:** the intersyndicale (joint union) strike on Thursday 5 Nov (CORROBORATED).
- **(d) 17 Oct:** a grievance about the policing of 17 Oct, or about how it was described, whichever side you take.
- **None:** nothing below requires you to act.

Then find your profile in section 6.

## 3. The case for not participating

This is the Abstainer's paragraph, as accepted in Round 3 and updated since:

> Staying home is a reasoned choice. 17 Oct brought 2,397 arrests (CORROBORATED, provisional), 123 people jailed, a hand torn off and an eye lost (CORROBORATED). Injured protesters were never counted (UNKNOWN). Kettling is lawful, so you only partly control your exposure. An arrest without charge still leaves an entry in the TAJ police file (CORROBORATED). For a non-EU resident, an arrest can trigger a review of their residence permit (CORROBORATED in law; how often it happens is UNKNOWN). No French evidence measures what one extra marcher changes, or one extra email to an MP either (UNKNOWN). The budget turns on votes, the 49.3 procedure and the arithmetic of a censure vote (CREDIBLE). Your absence costs the cause little; your arrest could cost you a lot. It needs no justification beyond your priorities and says nothing about the demands' merits.

This applies even to low-risk people who share a demand. Do not base your choice on either contested account of 17 Oct (section 9).

## 4. The case for participating

- **The window for (c) is real.** By any path, the budget stays open until about mid-December (CREDIBLE). If Part 1 passes, 5 Nov falls before the 17 Nov vote. A strike imposes an economic cost that a petition does not.
- **The government has responded in part.** It announced about 3,000 replacement teachers, who are TZR (roaming substitutes) moved from elsewhere rather than new hires (CREDIBLE). It also announced +€1.2bn for education (CORROBORATED as an announcement), and promised more decisions by the end of October (CORROBORATED; no date set).
- **Peaceful turnout has measured effects in the US** (Madestam et al. 2013; Wasow 2020; CORROBORATED as published studies). There is no French study, so the council gives no figure for the effect of one extra marcher.
- **Risk can be partly managed.** On 17 Oct, the stewarded main body of the march was calmer than the assembly points and the edges (CREDIBLE).

Notes to the table:
1. Dual nationals cannot be removed from France (CORROBORATED: basic nationality law).
2. Avoid at a 17 Oct flashpoint or after mid-afternoon.
3. Removal requires a "real, present and sufficiently serious" personal threat (CORROBORATED, CESEDA L251-1). A past conviction alone is not enough (CORROBORATED). Residents of 10+ years get stronger protection (CREDIBLE). Risk: low, not negligible.
4. Temporary card: can be withdrawn for a "threat to public order". 10-year card: only for a *serious* threat, which a single act may not meet (CORROBORATED in law). How often either happens is UNKNOWN.
5. A "serious threat" can push an asylum claim into the fast-track procedure (CREDIBLE; law-firm source).
6. Police custody (garde à vue) is possible from age 13 (CORROBORATED: juvenile justice code). Pupil injury counts conflict, at 200+ (CONTRADICTED). Youth sections were tear-gassed on 13 Oct (CREDIBLE). Several cities have curfews for minors. Only the ASSD grenade was suspended at lycéen protests, on 7 Oct; GMD dispersal grenades were not (CORROBORATED). Go with an adult.
7. Primary teachers must declare their strike by Monday 2 Nov. Pay deduction per strike day:
   - **State** civil service, including teachers: one indivisible thirtieth of monthly pay per day, even for a partial day (CORROBORATED).
   - **Local-authority (territoriale) and hospital** civil service: strictly proportional to the time off (CORROBORATED). ([Maire-info](https://www.maire-info.com/droit/greves-dans-la-fonction-publique-la-france-severement-epinglee-par-leurope-sur-la-regle-du-trentieme-indivisible-art26-27217), [Acteurs publics](https://acteurspublics.fr/articles/grevistes-de-la-fonction-publique-detat-la-justice-europeenne-censure-la-regle-du-trentieme))
8. Stricter devoir de réserve (duty of restraint), even off duty (CREDIBLE).
9. No strike notice is needed under a national call such as 5 Nov. Marching on a workday without striking counts as unpaid absence (CREDIBLE).

## 7. Where and when risk concentrates, plus a safety and kettling plan

On 17 Oct, trouble started at big-city assembly points around 12:00–14:00. It then moved to the front of the march, the youth sections, the edges, the dispersal and the night. City size did not predict it: Grenoble, Rennes and Brest all saw serious disorder (CREDIBLE).

**Legal status** (CORROBORATED: [Cour de cassation 21-81.054](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/juri/id/JURITEXT000045939979), [justice.fr](https://www.justice.fr/themes/manifestations-illicites)):
- **Undeclared:** joining is not an offence. Only the organisers are liable.
- **Banned** (by a prefectoral order issued in advance): a 4th-class fine. €135 standard, €375 increased, up to €750 if a court decides. One activist guide's figure of €1,500 is wrong (CONTRADICTED).
- **After the sommations (formal orders to disperse):** staying on purpose is a criminal offence (*délit*). Up to 1 year and €15,000; 3 years and €45,000 if your face is covered.
- **Face covering** in or beside a demonstration, to avoid being identified, is an offence in its own right (art. 431-9-1, 1 year and €15,000). A medical mask worn against tear gas is a grey zone.

**Checklist**
- [ ] Check the prefectoral orders (arrêtés) the evening before and on the morning. Bring a buddy, water, ID, and a lawyer's number written on your arm. Don't cover your face.
- [ ] Join after the march sets off and stay in the stewarded middle. Leave before dispersal, before dark, and before any curfew.
- [ ] **Phone: decide its role in advance.** If you go to march, leave your main phone at home or carry a spare with no personal data. If you go to document (strand d), use a dedicated device, film from the edge of the march rather than the front, and upload as you go. Refusing to give your unlock code can be an offence (CORROBORATED). Sign no consent to a search without a lawyer (CREDIBLE).
- [ ] TAJ: an arrest creates an entry even without charge, and it is not deleted automatically. You can ask the prosecutor to delete it (CORROBORATED).
- [ ] 5 Nov: rail unions are also striking (CORROBORATED). Plan to get home without SNCF trains, and before dark.
- [ ] Kettling: police must open an exit "as soon as circumstances allow" (CORROBORATED). Stay grouped and ask for the exit. Note the time, the place and the officers' ID numbers.
- [ ] Complaints (CORROBORATED; you can use more than one):
  - the [Défenseur des droits form](https://formulaire.defenseurdesdroits.fr). It does not pause legal deadlines.
  - a report to the IGPN (police) or IGGN (gendarmerie), filed under your own name.
  - a criminal complaint to the police or the procureur (prosecutor). This is the only route that can lead to prosecution.
  
  The LDH can help with any of these.

## 9. What the evidence says about 17 October

**The government's case that LFI was behind the unrest**, with the evidence it cites:
- Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez called the participation of LFI elected officials "irresponsible" (28 Sept, CORROBORATED quote).
- Around 1 Oct, the Prime Minister's office (Matignon) blamed "LFI and its far-left proxies" for an "organised manoeuvre" (CORROBORATED as a statement).
- It cites a 28 Sept intelligence note from the Paris police (DRPP), first reported by *Le Figaro* (right-leaning). The note describes far-left groups "instrumentalising" pupil unions (CREDIBLE that the note exists).
- *Le JDD* (right-wing, Bolloré-owned) reported that LFI local councillors are under investigation for transporting fireworks and mortars. Its sources are anonymous (ALLEGED).
- After 17 Oct, Nuñez said many far-left militants had been identified (CREDIBLE as a statement).
- Not in dispute: LFI openly supports the movement and shares its experience of running school blockades (CORROBORATED).
- Sources: [franceinfo explainer](https://www.franceinfo.fr/societe/education/blocage-des-lycees/pourquoi-lfi-est-elle-accusee-par-le-gouvernement-d-etre-a-l-origine-du-mouvement-lyceen-et-de-l-instrumentaliser_8221240.html) (public broadcaster), [JDD](https://www.lejdd.fr/politique/mobilisation-lyceenne-des-elus-lfi-impliques-dans-la-preparation-des-blocus-185739).

**What weighs against it:**
- franceinfo read the DRPP note. It covers only Seine-Saint-Denis: 27 actions, 6 of them with left-wing trade unionists or elected officials present.
- *Midi Libre* counts only 4 concrete LFI actions in it (CREDIBLE).
- The Paris prosecutor declined to confirm the "far-left" attribution (CREDIBLE).
- Mediapart's report, below, alleges pressure on intelligence officers.

**Assessment:** "LFI officials took part in some actions" is CORROBORATED. "LFI orchestrated the national movement" is ALLEGED: the government's own evidence covers one département.

## 10. Where the council disagreed

- **Abstainer vs Local Realist:** should someone who supports (c) stay home until 5 Nov, or decide city by city and hour by hour? The crux: how much control you have over your position in a crowd when kettling is lawful.
- **Abstainer vs Executor:** can a declared event on 24 Oct ever be a "Go"? The crux: whether it gives any leverage that 5 Nov does not (UNKNOWN).
- **Minors during the holidays:** the "Avoid" rating partly relied on a claim that grenades were re-authorised on 16 Oct, which is now graded UNKNOWN. The chair kept "Avoid" on the other grounds, which are weaker. In term time: "Caution", with no data.
- **Contrarian vs First Principles:** if 49.3 is used, does the size of the strike change the text? There is no French evidence either way.
- **Outsider vs Expansionist:** does strand (d) need street protest at all? The crux: whether the inquiry takes testimony from the public.

## 11. Limits of this report

- Facts come from search-engine summaries only, and nothing after 20 Oct is covered.
- Still UNKNOWN: the total number of injured protesters, how often residence permits are withdrawn after an arrest, and the level of risk in the main body of a march.

## 12. Glossary

- **49.3:** a constitutional article that passes a text without a vote unless a censure motion succeeds.
- **ASSD / GMD:** a launched stun grenade / a hand-thrown dispersal grenade.
- **Arrêté préfectoral:** a local order from the State, such as a ban or a curfew.
- **Censure:** a vote to bring down the government.
- **CESEDA:** the code governing foreigners' entry and residence.
- **Défenseur des droits:** the national rights ombudsman.
- **Devoir de réserve:** a public servant's duty of restraint.
- **DRPP:** the Paris police intelligence directorate.
- **IGPN / IGGN:** the police / gendarmerie inspectorates.
- **Intersyndicale:** the joint front of the main unions.
- **LDH:** the Human Rights League.
- **LFI:** La France insoumise, a left-wing party.
- **Lycéen:** a high-school pupil.
- **La Cimade / GISTI:** migrants' rights groups.
- **Mission:** a chapter of the budget.
- **Nasse:** kettling, where police encircle a crowd.
- **NPA / UNEF:** a far-left party / a student union.
- **Parcoursup:** the university admissions platform.
- **Part 1 (of the PLF):** the revenue half of the budget bill. If it is rejected, the whole bill falls.
- **PLF:** the annual budget bill.
- **Préfecture / préfet:** the State's local office / its representative.
- **Procureur:** the public prosecutor.
- **RT:** the territorial police intelligence service.
- **SNCF:** the national railway.
- **Sommations:** formal orders to disperse.
- **TAJ:** the police records file.
- **TZR:** a roaming substitute teacher.
- **USL:** a high-school pupils' union.
