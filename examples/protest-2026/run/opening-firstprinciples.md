# Opening position: First Principles

## 1. Position
Whether to "participate" is the wrong first question. Start with the outcome you want, then pick the cheapest action that reaches the place where that outcome is decided.
- **Wages, public services or budget (c), or schools (a):** march in the main body of the 5 Nov strike called by the intersyndicale (the joint union front), where the teachers' unions will also be.
- **Cost of living through the Gilets jaunes (b), 24 and 31 Oct:** there is no platform and no one to negotiate, so this is the weakest tool.
- **Anger about the 17 Oct policing (d):** a parliamentary inquiry now exists. Testimony and evidence are worth more there than a march.
- **None of these demands, or a high-cost profile such as a non-EU residence permit:** abstaining is the rational choice.

## 2. Arguments
1. **Leverage depends on timing.** The education budget is debated between 27 Oct and 16 Nov **[CREDIBLE]**, and the Assemblée votes on the whole budget on 17 Nov. 5 Nov lands before both (WG2 §1).
2. **Grievance (d) already has a better channel.** On 20 Oct every group backed giving the Assemblée's law committee inquiry powers: it can compel witnesses and take sworn testimony **[VERIFIED]** (WG1 §2). Evidence passed through the LDH (human rights league) reaches decision-makers. A march mostly adds arrest risk.
3. **The same act costs different people very different amounts.** Risk clusters at big-city assembly points from early afternoon, on the edges of marches and at night **[CREDIBLE]** (WG1 §5). An arrest goes into the TAJ (police records file), and that record is not deleted automatically **[VERIFIED]** (WG3 §2). For a non-EU resident it can threaten the residence permit **[VERIFIED law]** (WG3 §1).
