## 1. Removal risk depends on your status
- **EU/EEA/Swiss citizens.** An expulsion order (OQTF) needs *personal conduct* that is a "real, present and sufficiently serious threat to a fundamental interest of society" (CESEDA L251-1, from EU Directive 2004/38). Committing an offence, or even being convicted, is not enough on its own. Being arrested at a protest without a serious conviction almost certainly does not meet this test. **[VERIFIED rule; CREDIBLE that the risk is low]** — legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/section_lc/LEGITEXT000006070158/LEGISCTA000042771020/ ; Défenseure des droits opinion, 9 July 2019 (juridique.defenseurdesdroits.fr/doc_num.php?explnum_id=19046)
- **Non-EU residents with a residence permit.** Temporary and multi-year cards can be withdrawn or refused for a "threat to public order". The 10-year resident card can only be withdrawn for a *serious* threat. The 2024 immigration law (loi 2024-42) added a duty to respect "republican principles", and a serious breach can cost a permit. It also weakened protection against expulsion after certain convictions. An arrest alone is not automatic grounds, but prefectures check police files (§2). **This is the highest-stakes adult group.** **[VERIFIED law; CREDIBLE on practice]** — legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/article_jo/JORFARTI000049040337 ; gisti.org
- **Asylum seekers.** A prefect can move the claim to the fast-track procedure on "serious threat" grounds (CESEDA L531-27). Losing refugee status requires a very serious final conviction. **[CREDIBLE; this was not re-checked against official sources and the main source is a law firm]**
- **Dual nationals.** They cannot be removed. Stripping of nationality only applies to naturalised people, for terrorism-type offences. **[CREDIBLE]**

## 2. TAJ police file
Being arrested creates an entry in the file even if you are never prosecuted. If the case is dropped, the CPP art. 230-8 rule is that the data receive a *mention* (a note hiding them from administrative background checks) **unless the prosecutor orders deletion**. Deletion is not automatic. Lawyers report that a Cour de cassation ruling of 27 Jan 2026 confirmed there is no automatic deletion. That ruling was not seen. How to act: write to the prosecutor of the place where the case was handled. Ask the Interior Ministry for access to your entry and go to the CNIL if that is blocked. Background checks matter for jobs in private security, airport zones, police and justice, and for naturalisation and residence applications. **[VERIFIED core: the art. 230-8 text is quoted in CNIL decision SAN-2024-017. The "2 months, silence = refusal" timeline is CREDIBLE, blogs only]** — legifrance.gouv.fr/cnil/id/CNILTEXT000050449791 ; cnil.fr/fr/les-refus-dembauche-un-poste-dagent-de-securite-la-suite-dune-enquete-administrative

## 3. Your phone in police custody (GAV)
- **Unlock code.** In a full-court ruling of 7 Nov 2022 (n°21-83.146), the Cour de cassation held that a phone's unlock code can count as a "decryption key" when the phone has built-in encryption, which modern smartphones usually have. Refusing to give it after a formal request can then be an offence under Code pénal 434-15-2. How often protesters are prosecuted for this is **UNKNOWN**. **[VERIFIED: confirmed by the Economy Ministry legal directorate and several sources]** — economie.gouv.fr/daj/le-refus-de-communiquer-le-code-de-deverrouillage-dun-telephone-portable-peut-constituer-un
- **Consenting to a search.** The EU Court ruled on 4 Oct 2024 (C-548/21) that police need prior approval from a court or independent body to access phone data, except in urgent cases. Commentators report that on 19 May 2026 (n°25-87.563, a drug case from French Guiana) the Cour de cassation held that *signing written consent to a search of your belongings* under art. 76 CPP also counts as consent to read the phone. Sources disagree on whether the ruling was officially published (Bulletin vs unpublished). No official text was found. **[CREDIBLE: several commentators agree on what it held, but all are secondary]** — village-justice.com/articles/telephone-saisi-enquete-preliminaire-assentiment-fouille-emporte-consentement,57597.html ; clubic.com/actualite-614632 ; kohenavocats.com (…arret-19-mai-2026)
- Practical advice either way: leave your phone at home or bring a spare, memorise a lawyer's number, and don't sign a consent form before a lawyer has seen it. **[LDH practice advice]**

## 4. Kettling (nasse)
The Conseil d'État ruled on 10 June 2021 (n°444849, Lebon) and struck down the encirclement section of the 2020 policing doctrine (SNMO) because it did not say when the tactic could be used. **Correction:** the Interior Ministry reissued the doctrine on 16 Dec 2021, and encirclement is allowed again. Police may use it to prevent or stop "serious and imminent violence against persons or property". It must last only as long as strictly necessary, and a controlled exit point must be provided "as soon as circumstances allow". LDH says the criterion is too broad. MPs and the Défenseur des droits report long kettles with no exit. **Kettling is lawful and current: expect it.** **[VERIFIED]** — legifrance.gouv.fr/ceta/id/CETATEXT000043645883 ; ldh-france.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/202112_CP_SNMO.pdf ; LDH Aug 2026 rights sheet

## 6. Minors
- Children aged 13 and over can be held in police custody. Ages 10–13 can only be held under "retenue", which has strict limits. A lawyer is mandatory and parents must be informed. Parents are civilly liable for damage their child causes, but not criminally liable. **[CREDIBLE]**
- **Injuries:** the figures conflict. The Education Minister cited **190 pupils** injured. A franceinfo live blog headline gives **215 pupils, 85 school staff and ~715 police officers**, and Wikipedia cites an older figure of 119. Treat this as "about 200+ pupils by mid-October, depending on the date". Lens, 5 Oct: a 15-year-old lost a hand to a grenade. He denies picking it up. The Béthune prosecutor says it cannot yet be established whether it exploded in his hand or landed on him. The IGPN is investigating. Stun grenades were suspended at school protests on 7 Oct, but **on 16 Oct the Interior Minister said they could be used again in "extremely serious urban violence"**. **[CREDIBLE; figures CONTRADICTED among themselves]** — franceinfo.fr (…_8223883 ; …_8222821) ; fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouvement_lycéen_et_étudiant_de_2026_en_France

## 7. Public servants
You can march off duty. The duty of restraint (devoir de réserve) applies off duty too, and is stricter for senior, police and judicial staff. **Primary teachers** must send a strike declaration to their IEN at least 48h ahead, including one working day (Code de l'éducation L133-4). For Thu 5 Nov, send it on Mon 2 Nov, the first school day after Toussaint, to be safe. Pay deduction for State staff: 1/30 of the month per strike day, even for one hour. The commune must provide childcare cover if ≥25% of teachers strike. **[CREDIBLE: unions agree on all this; no official page was surfaced]** — se-unsa.org/2026/09/1er-degre-greve-intention-de-greve-sma/ ; snalc.fr/declaration-dintention-de-greve/

## 8. Lower-exposure alternatives
- **Contacting MPs:** US field experiments found contact moved legislators' votes on low-profile bills, and personalised messages worked better than templates. There is no French evidence. **[CREDIBLE for the US / UNKNOWN for France]**

A petition against "mutilating weapons" at school protests passed 100k on 11 Oct. The grenade suspension (§6) came from the Lens inquiry, not the petition. **[VERIFIED rules; CREDIBLE on facts]** — petitions.assemblee-nationale.fr

## Glossary
- **CESEDA**: the French code on foreigners' entry, residence and asylum.
- **OQTF**: an order to leave French territory.
- **Titre de séjour / carte de résident**: a residence permit / the 10-year resident card.
- **GAV (garde à vue)**: police custody (24h, can be extended).
- **CPP**: Code of Criminal Procedure.
- **TAJ**: the national police file of people involved in investigations.
- **Mention**: a note on a TAJ entry that hides it from background checks.
- **CNIL**: the data-protection authority.
- **CJEU**: the EU Court of Justice.
- **Cour de cassation**: France's top criminal and civil court.
- **Conseil d'État**: France's top administrative court.
- **Nasse**: kettling, where police encircle a crowd.
- **SNMO**: the national public-order policing doctrine.
- **LDH**: the Human Rights League (runs legal observers and publishes rights guides).
- **IGPN**: the police internal-affairs inspectorate.
- **IEN**: primary-school inspector (a teacher's line manager).
- **Devoir de réserve**: civil servants' duty of restraint in public expression.
- **Retenue**: short, limited holding of a 10–13-year-old.
- **Classement sans suite**: the prosecutor drops the case.
