# Opening position — Executor

## 1. Position
Don't decide on "the movement". Pick one date. Your options this week, conditional on your demands:
- **Wages, public services or teachers' demands (strand c):** go on **Thu 5 Nov**, the intersyndicale (joint national union call). Walk in the declared march, by day, in its main body.
- **Cost of living (strand b):** go on **Sat 24 Oct** only if your préfecture has confirmed the march is declared. In Paris that is still undecided.
- **Non-EU residents and minors:** avoid anything undeclared or held in the evening.
- **Abstaining:** a legitimate choice. Missing this week costs you nothing on 5 Nov.

## 2. Arguments
1. **The risk can be planned around.** On 17 Oct, trouble clustered at big-city assembly points from midday, then at the march's edges, at dispersal and after dark. The calm main body was safer [CREDIBLE, WG1 §5]. There were 2,397 arrests and confirmed serious injuries [VERIFIED]. So join after the march sets off, leave before dispersal, and be home before curfews start: 22:00 for minors in Bordeaux, 23:00 in Paris [CREDIBLE, WG1 §5].
2. **Checklist:**
   - Check the préfecture's orders (arrêtés) the evening before and again that morning.
   - Leave your phone at home, or carry a spare. Refusing to give your phone's unlock code can be an offence [VERIFIED].
   - Sign no consent form without a lawyer [CREDIBLE, WG3 §3].
   - Memorise a lawyer's number.
   - Expect kettling (being encircled by police), which is legal [VERIFIED, WG3 §4]. Agree a meeting point with a friend in case you get separated.
   - **Primary teachers:** send your strike declaration to your IEN (inspector) on Mon 2 Nov [CREDIBLE, WG3 §7].
3. **Lower-risk options:** write a personal message to your MP during the spending debate (27 Oct–16 Nov), or sign an Assemblée petition. Neither carries any arrest risk. Whether they work in France is UNKNOWN [WG2 §1, WG3 §8].
