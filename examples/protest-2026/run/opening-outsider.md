# OPENING POSITION — Outsider

## 1. Position
There is no single "movement". There are four strands. Decide which demand you actually share, then:
- **Wages, public services or the budget (5 Nov strike):** national unions organise it, with stewards and routes normally declared in advance (5 Nov routes not yet published). If this is your demand, join the main body of the march in daylight.
- **Fuel and the cost of living (Gilets jaunes, 24 and 31 Oct):** go only if your préfecture (the local state office) confirms the event is declared, not banned and not under a curfew.
- **Schools:** pupils can follow their school's union calls. Others are supporters, not the core.
- **Anger over the 17 Oct policing:** a march is a poor fit for this grievance. A parliamentary inquiry with the power to compel witnesses now exists (WG1 §2, VERIFIED).

If none of these demands is yours, staying home is a fully coherent choice.

## 2. Arguments
**A. Different strands carry different risks.** Each strand has its own organisers and dates (WG2 §2, VERIFIED/CREDIBLE). The 17 Oct violence clustered at big-city assembly points and after dark (WG1 §5, CREDIBLE). Judging a daytime union march by 17 Oct is a mistake.

**B. The budget deadline may be outdated.** The education budget is debated only if part 1 of the budget (the revenue side) passes in today's vote (WG2 §1, CREDIBLE). The finance committee rejected part 1 on 9 Oct by 31 votes to 3, and last year's solemn vote failed 1–404 ([legifiscal](https://www.legifiscal.fr/actualites-fiscales/4684-plf-2027-commission-finances-rejette-partie-recettes-creuse-deficit-environ-10-md.html); CREDIBLE). So "march before the education vote" may point at a debate that never happens. The nearer pressure point is Lecornu's promised end-of-October decisions (WG2 §4, VERIFIED).

**C. The jargon hides real costs that first-timers rarely know:**
- An arrest creates an entry in the TAJ (the national police file) even if the case is dropped (WG3 §2, VERIFIED).
- Non-EU residents risk losing their residence permit (WG3 §1, VERIFIED law).
- Refusing to give police your phone unlock code can be an offence (WG3 §3, VERIFIED).
