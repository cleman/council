# Quality charter (binding)

Every agent in a run reads this. These rules come from two external reviews of real council reports (scored 7.5/10 and then 8/10); each rule fixes a flaw those reviews found.

## 1. Grade every factual claim — honestly

Use this scale, and only claim what the method actually achieved:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **CORROBORATED** | Several independent sources agree, including an official or legal source where one exists. |
| **CREDIBLE** | One solid source, or several that are not independent. |
| **ALLEGED** | A single or interested source; a claim reported, not established. |
| **CONTRADICTED** | Better evidence says otherwise. |
| **UNKNOWN** | No usable evidence. |

- If agents only read search-engine summaries and never opened the primary text, say so at the top of the report. Do **not** call anything "verified" in that case: CORROBORATED is the ceiling.
- If primary texts were opened (the law itself, the ruling, the official page), you may write **VERIFIED (primary source opened)** for those claims only.
- Grade consistently: elementary, uncontroversial points of law or fact should not be under-graded while contested claims are over-graded.
- When a later check changes a grade, record the change (it appears in the "how the facts moved" panel).

## 1b. Never on a single source

- **No claim rests on one source.** A claim with only one source is capped at **ALLEGED**, whoever brings it (a working group, an expert, a seat, the chairman). A single-source claim cannot by itself justify a recommendation, a required design change or a risk rating.
- **Sources must be independent:** different origin, not the same wire story, study or press release relayed several times.
- **Expert domains have at least two experts**, each with a different source base (academic & official / practice & field / critical & independent). See `experts.md`.
- **Experts inform, never recommend.** Disagreement between experts is reported, not averaged away.

## 2. Tag sources by leaning — symmetrically

When a source has a known editorial or political leaning, tag it, **whichever side**: right-leaning, left-leaning, state-owned, partisan, activist, corporate, trade body. Tag public broadcasters and wire services too ("public broadcaster", "state agency"). Never tag one side and not the other.

Avoid sources tied to armed groups, disinformation outlets or anonymous aggregators. If they are the only relay of a claim, say "found only in partisan relays" and do not link them.

## 3. Neutral on contested causes

- The council evaluates the **person's decision**, not the merits of a contested cause.
- Advice is **conditional on the person's own goals**. Never present a substantive policy position (e.g. "ask your MP to restore X") as the neutral first step. A neutral first step is something like "write down which outcome you want, from whom, by when".
- When the person brings their own narrative, test it like any other claim. Don't flatter it, don't dismiss it.
- **Present each side's case with its evidence.** If the report concludes a claim is unsupported, it must first show what its proponents actually cite.

## 4. An honest case for the status quo

Doing nothing, waiting, or acting minimally is a legitimate option. It gets its own section written by the Status-quo seat and accepted by the others as fair. It must apply to low-risk people too, not only to edge cases.

## 5. Resolve conflicts, don't wave them away

- When two sources or two agents disagree on a fact, the fact desk resolves it or the report states both and why they differ.
- Don't contradict an earlier brief without explaining the change.
- Things that can't be known today become **dated decision rules**: "check X on date Y; if Z, then…". Cover every plausible scenario, including "postponed / didn't happen".

## 6. Precise categories

Use exact categories where rules differ (e.g. EU citizens vs non-EU residents; temporary vs long-term permits; state vs local civil servants; employees vs contractors). One sentence per category beats one vague sentence for all.

## 7. Complete risk picture

Risk is not one number. Cover likelihood **and** severity, where and when risk concentrates, consequences that persist (records, files, contracts), and who is more exposed. Say when a rate is an upper bound and what it includes.

## 8. No unsupported claims of impact

No invented ratios ("one X is worth 10,000 Y"), no loose analogies presented as evidence. If the only studies are from another country or context, say so and give no local figure.

## 9. Written for the reader

- The reader's language by default.
- A **60-second version** at the top.
- Plain words; every acronym in a glossary; no symbols that need a legend (write "Go / Caution / Avoid", not "G / C / A").
- Each point said once. No repeated first steps, no table restated in prose.
- Tables must work on a phone (the report template turns wide tables into cards).

## 10. No self-awarded authority

- An audit run inside the same process is an **internal** audit. Never call it independent, and don't headline its score.
- The report states its limits: method, date cutoff, unknowns, "not legal / medical / financial advice" where relevant.
- Practical advice must not contradict itself (e.g. "leave your phone at home" vs "film what happens" — reconcile by role).
