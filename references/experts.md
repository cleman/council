# Expert panels

Seats argue. Experts **inform**. An expert never recommends what the person should do, never takes a side on a contested cause, and never speaks from a single source.

## Why experts are separate from seats and working groups

| Role | Takes a position? | When it works | What it produces |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seat | Yes: argues from its angle | Openings, rooms | Positions, arguments |
| Working group | No | Once, at the start | The shared factual base |
| Expert | **No** | **On call** throughout the run | Answers to precise questions, with how settled each answer is |
| Fact desk | No | Between rounds | Resolution of conflicts across domains; dated rules for unknowables |

All experts run on the same model family, so the "expert" label adds nothing by itself. What makes an expert worth hearing is **the sources it is bound to and the rule that it can't stand on one of them alone.**

## The two-source rule (binding)

1. **Each domain has at least two experts**, each assigned a **different source base**:

   | Source base | Typical sources |
   |---|---|
   | **Academic & official** | peer-reviewed studies, meta-analyses, statutes, case law, regulators, statistical offices, standards bodies |
   | **Practice & field** | professional bodies, practitioner guides, trade press, case reports, field data, experienced practitioners' published methods |
   | **Critical & independent** (add for contested domains) | independent researchers, watchdogs, NGOs, investigative outlets, dissenting experts, replication studies |

   Adapt the bases to the domain (e.g. for UX: "research literature" / "industry practice & benchmarks" / "accessibility & user advocacy").

2. **Each claim rests on at least two independent sources.** Independence means different origin, not the same wire story or the same study relayed by several outlets. A claim with only one source is capped at **ALLEGED**, whoever brings it.

3. **Experts answer separately, in parallel.** They don't see each other's answers before submitting.

4. **Disagreement between experts is a finding.** Report "the field is divided: A says…, B says…, because…". Don't average it away, and don't let the chairman pick a winner without saying why.

5. **Experts state the strength of the evidence**, not just the answer: consensus / majority view / contested / emerging / unknown, plus the grade of each claim (quality charter §1).

6. **No recommendations.** An expert may say "the evidence on X is strong" or "practice usually does Y". It may not say "you should do Y".

## Choosing domains

In triage, list the knowledge domains the question depends on. Pick the 1–4 that would change the answer if they were wrong. Examples:

- *Should I join a protest?* → law (public order, immigration), policing practice, the political calendar.
- *Should we launch product X?* → market data, unit economics, regulation.
- *Design a running training method* → exercise physiology, coaching practice, injury prevention / physiotherapy, nutrition.
- *Design the website for it* → UX research, accessibility, data & privacy.

## Sizing by level

| Level | Domains × experts |
|---|---|
| Quick | none (seats only) |
| Standard | 1 domain × 2 |
| Deep | 2–3 domains × 2 |
| Full | 3–4 domains × 2–3 |

The configuration proposal lists the domains and their source bases, so the user can add, remove or rename one before the run.

## When experts are heard

- **Opening hearing** (optional, Deep/Full): before the seats' openings, each domain's experts answer the 3–5 questions the triage flagged as decisive. Their answers go to every seat alongside the working-group reports.
- **On request:** any room may ask for a hearing. Put the question under "Expert requests" instead of "Information requests". The orchestrator sends it to the right domain's experts in parallel.
- **Cross-examination:** in the next round, seats may challenge an expert answer. The orchestrator sends the challenge back to the same experts once.
